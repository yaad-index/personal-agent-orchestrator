# ADR 0046: Channel ownership, who may command a channel

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-26)

Supersedes ADR-0026 (operator commands from chat): keeps every one of its decisions and changes one, who may issue a channel's steering commands. They are no longer the operator's alone; **channel ownership**, a per-channel grant on the access matrix, lets a non-operator steer a channel beside the operator, while agent lifecycle and the gate's approval answer stay the operator's. Extends ADR-0004 (the gate), ADR-0005 (access and management surface), ADR-0009 (channels), ADR-0010 (message model), ADR-0012 (the access matrix), ADR-0019 (sender attribution), ADR-0022 (operator subjects) and ADR-0038 (a group's topics are conversations of that group, which superseded ADR-0023's agent I/O and channel bindings), and builds on ADR-0045 (channel membership), whose deferred ownership half this ADR draws.

## Context

ADR-0026 let an operator steer agents and answer the gate from chat, and made every steering command the operator's: binding an agent's output, the view controls, a channel's default agent, an agent's start, stop and restart, and the gate's approval answer were all for a subject routed as the operator (ADR-0026 point 3). ADR-0045 split a channel's access into two halves and drew the first, **membership**, who may read and participate, and deferred the second, **ownership**, who may command the channel, to a following ADR, because changing ADR-0026 point 3 touches an Accepted ADR and the index requires that to be a full supersession, never a partial edit.

This is that following ADR. It supersedes ADR-0026 in full, keeps every decision it made, and changes only who may steer: a channel's steering is no longer the operator's alone but is also open to a non-operator granted **ownership** of that channel, while agent lifecycle (start, stop, restart) and the gate's approval answer stay the operator's. Where ADR-0026 cited ADR-0023, this ADR carries the citation to ADR-0038, which superseded ADR-0023 (keeping its points 1, 2 and 6 to 8, changing 3 to 5).

## Decision

1. **`/pao` belongs to pao and never reaches an agent** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 1). A message whose first word is `/pao` is a command to pao. The communication layer handles it before routing (ADR-0009 point 4). It is never delivered to an agent, whatever the channel, the routing precedence of ADR-0038 point 4, or the sender's sticky target. A `/pao` command pao does not recognize is answered with usage, not passed on. Addressing an agent through `/pao agent <name> <message>` (ADR-0038 point 6) is one of these commands: pao reads it, then routes the message.

2. **A chat command is an existing node operation, not a new one** (from ADR-0026 point 2, with the measure made the issuer's own grants). Each command maps to an operation the node already exposes at its operator surface, such as the stream binding, the view controls, a channel's or conversation's default agent (ADR-0038 point 5), an agent's start, stop and restart, and the gate's answer. It runs through the same checks as the equivalent operation from the command line, measured against **the issuer's own grants**, so a command can do nothing from chat that its issuer's grants would not allow at the operator surface. Measuring against the issuer rather than against the operator is what lets a non-operator owner do anything at all. Answers and failures read the same either way.

3. **Changed from ADR-0026 point 3: who may issue a channel's steering commands is its owner; steering is no longer the operator's alone.** Who may run a command is still decided per command from the sender's subject, attributed by the channel's identity binding (ADR-0019 point 3). Steering splits in two. **Channel-scoped** commands, binding an agent's output to a channel, setting the default agent of that channel or of a conversation within it (ADR-0038 point 5), and the view controls on it, are for the channel's **owner**: the **operator** may steer every channel by virtue of the operator role (ADR-0022), exactly as in ADR-0026, which is a role and not an ownership grant held over every channel; and a **non-operator** may be granted **ownership of a specific channel**, a per-channel resource grant on the access matrix (ADR-0012), and may then steer that channel. A conversation's default is owned with the channel the conversation belongs to. **Agent-lifecycle** commands, an agent's start, stop and restart, are **not** opened by channel ownership nor by mere access to the agent, and stay the **operator's** for now, with a dedicated lifecycle grant left to later work (below); this stops a subject that may only address an agent from being able to stop it. Every command stays limited to the agents the subject reaches (ADR-0012). This **changes** ADR-0026 point 3, which made steering the operator's alone: channel steering is now also a non-operator channel owner's, while agent lifecycle and the gate answer (point 4) stay the operator's; it is not a narrowing, since an owner need not be the operator. ADR-0038 point 5 attributes a default-switch to a steering command it cites to ADR-0026; that citation now resolves to this ADR, and the command is the channel owner's. This is the per-resource command authority ADR-0022 point 6 anticipated the matrix would decide. **Until the matrix can record per-channel ownership (left open below), only the operator steers, by role, exactly as ADR-0026, and no non-operator ownership exists yet**, so nothing is granted automatically and ADR-0012 point 2's default-deny holds. A subject that is neither the operator nor the channel's owner may use `/pao help` and address the agents it may reach; any other command from it is refused as unknown, not as forbidden, so the refusal does not reveal what exists (ADR-0012 point 4).

4. **Answering an approval question stays the operator's, within the agents the operator reaches, and ownership does not grant it** (ADR-0026 point 3's gate-answer clause, kept with the operator and separated from steering). An approval question (ADR-0004) must reach whoever may answer it, the operator, and never a non-operator subject (ADR-0038 point 7), and the operator answers only for the agents it reaches under the matrix (ADR-0012), the same limit ADR-0026 point 3 placed on it. Channel ownership grants steering only; it does not grant answering the gate. So a channel's owner who is not the operator may steer that channel but cannot answer its approval prompts, which still reach the operator.

5. **Administration stays at the operator surface** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 4). Commands that change who may do what are not offered in chat: grants, subjects and identity bindings, invitations, the operator flag (ADR-0022 point 2), channels and the node's configuration. **Granting or revoking channel ownership is itself such a change and stays off chat**, so a chat account that is taken over cannot widen its own access, including making itself a channel's owner. These stay where ADR-0005 point 3 puts them.

6. **A command's answer goes to the sender, on the channel it came from** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 5). The reply to a command, and any refusal, is sent only to the subject who issued it, on the channel it arrived on, never fanned out to the other channels an agent's output reaches (ADR-0038 point 7). An agent's output that a command causes, such as a backtrack, goes where that agent's output is bound.

7. **`/pao help` is built from the command table and shows only what the sender may run** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 6). Help lists each command with its usage and one line on what it does, from the same table pao dispatches from, so it cannot list a command that does not exist or miss one that does. It is filtered by points 3 and 4, so a subject is shown only the channel-steering commands it owns a channel to run, and the agent-lifecycle commands and the gate answer only if it is the operator. A command's usage is shown when it is given wrong arguments.

8. **Buttons are a shortcut for a typed command, never a separate path** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 7). Where a platform has action buttons, pao may attach them to its messages (ADR-0010 point 5 prefers buttons for a choice): allow, deny and always on an approval question, pause and resume on a bound agent's output. A button carries the command it stands for; a press is handled exactly as that typed command from the person who pressed it, attributed by the platform's authenticated sender of the press (ADR-0019 point 2), not by whom the message was sent to, and re-checked under point 3 (and under point 4 for the gate answer) when it arrives. A channel without buttons offers the same actions as typed commands only; every action has a typed command.

9. **A command's effect lasts as long as the same change made at the operator surface** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 8). A change made from chat is kept, or lost on restart, exactly as the same change made at the operator surface is. Whether a stream binding survives a node restart is a property of the binding, not of where it was set.

10. **Addressing uses only pao's own syntax** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 9). An agent is addressed as `/pao agent <name> <message>`, which ADR-0038 point 6 keeps as the base form, a tag as `/pao tag <tag> <message>`, and `/pao <name> <message>` is the short form for an agent. This ADR **decides** the tag form and the short form that ADR-0038 point 6 still leaves deferred, and **replaces** the `@name` and `#tag` prefixes of the first build. pao's command words are reserved and no agent may take one as its name. No form reuses a platform's own mention syntax; a leading `@name` or `#tag` is ordinary text, routed like any message that names no target (ADR-0038 point 4).

## What this ADR deliberately does NOT decide

- How a non-operator is granted ownership of a channel and how it is recorded on the matrix. Until that lands, point 3 keeps steering the operator's by role (ADR-0026) with no non-operator ownership yet; what a pending grant or a revocation does is left to the matrix work.
- A dedicated grant for agent lifecycle (start, stop, restart), which point 3 keeps operator-only for now rather than opening it to anyone who may address the agent.
- Whether the operator flag (ADR-0022) is retired in favour of per-resource ownership once the matrix lands, which ADR-0022 point 6 leaves to the matrix work.
- The exact command words and argument forms beyond the addressing forms of point 10, which operations are in the first build, whether bindings and levels persist across a restart, the button layout per platform, how a platform's own command menu lists `/pao`, and recording chat commands in an audit log, all as ADR-0026 left them.

## Consequences

This supersedes ADR-0026 in full. Its one substantive change is point 3: a channel's steering is no longer the operator's alone. The operator keeps steering every channel by role, as in ADR-0026, and channel ownership adds a per-channel grant on the access matrix by which a non-operator may steer a channel; agent lifecycle and the gate answer stay the operator's, and point 4 states plainly that the gate answer is not part of ownership. Everything else ADR-0026 decided is carried forward unchanged, with its ADR-0023 citations moved to ADR-0038, which superseded ADR-0023. Together with ADR-0045, a channel now has two separable grants resting on the access matrix (ADR-0012) over its default-deny (ADR-0012 point 2): **membership** (ADR-0045), who may read and participate, and **ownership** (here), who may command. Once the matrix records ownership, a channel can be steered by a non-operator who owns it, while the gate's approval still reaches only the operator, and administration, including granting ownership itself, stays at the operator surface, so chat adds a way in but no new authority. This builds on ADR-0005, ADR-0009, ADR-0010, ADR-0012, ADR-0019, ADR-0022, ADR-0004 and ADR-0038, and on ADR-0045 whose deferred ownership half it draws; it supersedes ADR-0026.
