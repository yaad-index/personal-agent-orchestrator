# ADR 0046: Channel ownership, who may command a channel

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-26)

Supersedes ADR-0026 (operator commands from chat): keeps every one of its decisions and changes one, who may issue a channel's steering commands, from the operator as such to the channel's **owner**, a per-channel grant on the access matrix. Extends ADR-0005 (access and management surface), ADR-0009 (channels), ADR-0010 (message model), ADR-0012 (the access matrix), ADR-0022 (operator subjects) and ADR-0038 (agent I/O and channel bindings, which superseded ADR-0023), and builds on ADR-0045 (channel membership), whose deferred ownership half this ADR draws.

## Context

ADR-0026 let an operator steer agents and answer the gate from chat, and made every steering command the operator's: binding an agent's output, the view controls, a channel's default agent, an agent's start, stop and restart, and the gate's approval answer were all for a subject routed as the operator (ADR-0026 point 3). ADR-0045 split a channel's access into two halves and drew the first, **membership**, who may read and participate, and deferred the second, **ownership**, who may command the channel, to a following ADR, because changing ADR-0026 point 3 touches an Accepted ADR and the index requires that to be a full supersession, never a partial edit.

This is that following ADR. It supersedes ADR-0026 in full, keeps every decision it made, and changes only who may steer: from the operator as such to a per-channel **owner**. Answering an approval question is deliberately left with the operator rather than folded into ownership. Where ADR-0026 cited ADR-0023, this ADR carries the citation to ADR-0038, which superseded ADR-0023 (keeping its points 1, 2 and 6 to 8, changing 3 to 5).

## Decision

1. **`/pao` belongs to pao and never reaches an agent** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 1). A message whose first word is `/pao` is a command to pao. The communication layer handles it before routing (ADR-0009 point 4). It is never delivered to an agent, whatever the channel, the routing precedence of ADR-0038 point 4, or the sender's sticky target. A `/pao` command pao does not recognize is answered with usage, not passed on. Addressing an agent through `/pao agent <name> <message>` (ADR-0038 point 6) is one of these commands: pao reads it, then routes the message.

2. **A chat command is an existing node operation, not a new one** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 2). Each command maps to an operation the node already exposes at its operator surface, such as the stream binding, the view controls, a channel's or conversation's default agent (ADR-0038 point 5), an agent's start, stop and restart, and the gate's answer. It runs through the same checks as from the command line, and can do nothing from chat that its issuer could not do at the operator surface. Answers and failures read the same either way.

3. **Changed from ADR-0026 point 3: who may steer a channel is its owner, a per-channel grant.** Steering commands, which change a binding, a view, a default (ADR-0038 point 5) or an agent's state, are for a subject granted **ownership of that channel**, a per-channel resource grant on the access matrix (ADR-0012), not for the operator as such. They stay limited to the agents that subject reaches under the matrix (ADR-0012). The owner may be the operator, and commonly is, but ownership is granted per channel, a subject owns only the channels it is granted, and no subject owns a channel by default (ADR-0012 point 2). A subject that does not own the channel may use `/pao help` and address the agents it may reach; any other command from it is refused as unknown, not as forbidden, so the refusal does not reveal what exists (ADR-0012 point 4). This narrows ADR-0026 point 3's "the operator" to "the channel's owner", and is the per-resource command authority ADR-0022 point 6 anticipated the matrix would decide.

4. **Answering an approval question stays the operator's, and ownership does not grant it** (ADR-0026 point 3's gate-answer clause, kept with the operator and separated from steering). An approval question (ADR-0004) must reach whoever may answer it, the operator, and never a non-operator subject (ADR-0038 point 7). Channel ownership grants steering only; it does not grant answering the gate. So a channel's owner who is not the operator may steer that channel but cannot answer its approval prompts, which still reach the operator.

5. **Administration stays at the operator surface** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 4). Commands that change who may do what are not offered in chat: grants, subjects and identity bindings, invitations, the operator flag (ADR-0022 point 2), channels and the node's configuration. **Granting or revoking channel ownership is itself such a change and stays off chat**, so a chat account that is taken over cannot widen its own access, including making itself a channel's owner. These stay where ADR-0005 point 3 puts them.

6. **A command's answer goes to the sender, on the channel it came from** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 5). The reply to a command, and any refusal, is sent only to the subject who issued it, on the channel it arrived on, never fanned out to the other channels an agent's output reaches (ADR-0038 point 7). An agent's output that a command causes, such as a backtrack, goes where that agent's output is bound.

7. **`/pao help` is built from the command table and shows only what the sender may run** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 6). Help lists each command with its usage and one line on what it does, from the same table pao dispatches from, so it cannot list a command that does not exist or miss one that does. It is filtered by point 3, so a subject is shown only the steering commands it owns a channel to run, and the gate answer only if it is the operator (point 4).

8. **Buttons are a shortcut for a typed command, never a separate path** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 7). Where a platform has action buttons, pao may attach them to its messages (ADR-0010 point 5 prefers buttons for a choice): allow, deny and always on an approval question, pause and resume on a bound agent's output. A button carries the command it stands for; a press is handled exactly as that typed command from the person who pressed it, attributed by the platform's authenticated sender of the press (ADR-0019 point 2), and re-checked under point 3 (and under point 4 for the gate answer) when it arrives. A channel without buttons offers the same actions as typed commands only; every action has a typed command.

9. **A command's effect lasts as long as the same change made at the operator surface** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 8). A change made from chat is kept, or lost on restart, exactly as the same change made at the operator surface is. Whether a stream binding survives a node restart is a property of the binding, not of where it was set.

10. **Addressing uses only pao's own syntax** (unchanged from ADR-0026 point 9). An agent is addressed as `/pao agent <name> <message>` and a tag as `/pao tag <tag> <message>`, with `/pao <name> <message>` the short form for an agent (ADR-0038 point 6). pao's command words are reserved and no agent may take one as its name. No form reuses a platform's own mention syntax; a leading `@name` or `#tag` is ordinary text, routed like any message that names no target (ADR-0038 point 4).

## What this ADR deliberately does NOT decide

- How channel ownership is granted and recorded on the matrix, and a channel's default owner at creation: whether the operator owns every channel until ownership is granted away, or a channel starts unowned.
- Whether the operator flag (ADR-0022) is retired in favour of per-resource ownership once the matrix lands, which ADR-0022 point 6 leaves to the matrix work.
- The exact command words and argument forms beyond the addressing forms of point 10, which operations are in the first build, whether bindings and levels persist across a restart, the button layout per platform, and recording chat commands in an audit log, all as ADR-0026 left them.

## Consequences

This supersedes ADR-0026 in full. Its one substantive change is point 3: steering a channel is the channel owner's, a per-channel grant on the access matrix, rather than the operator's as such, and point 4 states plainly that the gate answer is not part of ownership and stays with the operator. Everything else ADR-0026 decided is carried forward unchanged, with its ADR-0023 citations moved to ADR-0038, which superseded ADR-0023. Together with ADR-0045, a channel now has two separable grants resting on the access matrix (ADR-0012) over its default-deny (ADR-0012 point 2): **membership** (ADR-0045), who may read and participate, and **ownership** (here), who may command. A channel can therefore be steered by someone who is not the operator, while the gate's approval still reaches only the operator, and administration, including granting ownership itself, stays at the operator surface, so chat adds a way in but no new authority. This builds on ADR-0005, ADR-0009, ADR-0010, ADR-0012, ADR-0019, ADR-0022, ADR-0004 and ADR-0038, and on ADR-0045 whose deferred ownership half it draws; it supersedes ADR-0026.
