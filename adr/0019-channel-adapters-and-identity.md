# ADR 0019: Channel adapters and sender identity

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-14)

## Context

ADR-0009 fixed what a channel is (the human-facing subset of sources) and that every source is an adapter in one place, but left each adapter's implementation, and which platforms ship first, open. ADR-0012 made humans subjects in the access matrix but explicitly deferred how an inbound sender is resolved to a particular human subject. ADR-0011 fixed how a backend is made pluggable, a uniform contract with the profile declaring the specifics, without binding pao's core to any one backend. This ADR fills those two gaps for channels: how a channel adapter is added, and how an inbound sender on a channel becomes a known human subject.

## Decision

1. **A channel adapter is compiled in behind one uniform contract.** A channel adapter implements the channel-source contract of ADR-0009 (inbound normalized to a sender-tagged message, outbound through the one conduit), the way ADR-0011 holds every backend to one uniform contract and keeps pao's core neutral. Adding a channel is writing an adapter to that contract and wiring it into the core; it ships by rebuilding pao, not by loading code at runtime, and runtime configuration only selects and configures the adapters already compiled in. How an adapter is wired in, an explicit core-side registry or self-registration, is left open, as ADR-0011 leaves backend registration open. Two chat platforms ship first; the contract is platform-generic, so a further platform is a new adapter and not a change to pao's core, and which platforms ship first stays open as ADR-0009 left it.

2. **Every inbound message carries an authenticated per-sender id, in every channel, including a one-to-one.** The adapter tags each message with the platform's authenticated sender field, the value the platform itself vouches for as who sent the message, never one read from the message text or a forwarded or quoted part. This per-sender id is distinct from the conversation or endpoint id that names where a reply goes: in a group the conversation id is shared while the sender id is not, so identity (point 3) keys on the per-sender id and never on the conversation. Carrying it unconditionally, rather than only when a conversation has more than one participant, is what lets one conversation shape cover both the one-to-one and the group case.

3. **Identity is a binding from (channel, per-sender id) to one human subject, created at registration and revocable by the operator.** The pair of a channel and a per-sender id resolves to exactly one human subject of ADR-0012. The binding is established at registration, and registration is where the human is authenticated; after it, an inbound message is attributed to its subject by looking the pair up, with no per-message auth. The resolved subject, not the external id, is the identity that backs the sender field the agent reads, with how that field renders left to ADR-0010; the external id stays orchestrator-side like the source kind (ADR-0001). A binding is revocable by the operator, since platform ids are recycled and accounts change hands; once revoked, the pair falls back to unbound (point 5). This is the sender-to-subject resolution ADR-0012 left open.

4. **One channel serves many subjects, and one subject is reachable on many channels.** Because the binding keys on the per-sender id and not on the channel alone, a single channel (one bot on one platform) carries several humans, each told apart by their own id and each its own subject. Symmetrically, one human may hold a binding on several channels, the same subject reached on two platforms, each binding independent. A channel is therefore not a human; it is a front on which bindings live.

5. **An unbound sender reaches only registration, and learns nothing from it.** An external sender id with no binding resolves to no subject, and under the default-deny of ADR-0005 and ADR-0012 reaches nothing; consistent with ADR-0012 it is not distinguishable from a sender that does not exist, so it cannot probe for what is present. The one surface an unbound sender can reach is the registration entry point itself, whose response is identical for every unbound id, whether or not that id is already bound to someone, so registration reveals no subject, agent, resource, or existing binding. Registration is the only way a sender gains a subject.

6. **A conversation shared by several humans is the same shape with more configuration, and denies by default.** A group conversation is one channel endpoint whose inbound messages carry different per-sender ids, each resolving to its own subject by point 3, with attribution per message by point 2. What it adds over a one-to-one is configuration, not a new mechanism: which subjects the conversation admits, and how a resource scope (the tags of ADR-0012) applies to a message whose readers are several subjects at once. Until that configuration exists, a group denies by default: an unconfigured group admits no one and grants nothing, and a message visible to several readers is bounded by the intersection of their scopes, so a shared conversation is the narrowest reader set and never the widest. The group configuration is deferred here, and the multi-human conversation is deliberately the more-configured case.

7. **A rejected inbound is recorded in full, bounded, for later inspection, never silently dropped.** When a channel delivers a message pao does not act on, because it resolves to no subject (point 5) or falls outside the sender's access (ADR-0012), pao records the attempt with full detail: the channel, the external sender id, the raw payload, the reason it was rejected, and the time. Because that payload comes from an unauthenticated stranger, the record is bounded, rate-limited or coalesced per sender so a flood cannot fill disk, and it is readable only by the operator so it does not become an exposed pile of stranger content. The record is inert: a rejected inbound never reaches an agent, directly or through any automatic path, and becomes agent input only by an explicit operator decision, since routing unauthenticated stranger content to an agent without a human in the loop is itself the security risk this guards. A registration attempt, the one surface an unbound sender reaches (point 5), is recorded the same bounded way. That record is the audit role foreseen in ADR-0002; this ADR fixes that these are among the things the role captures, and leaves the record's storage, retention, inspection surface, and the exact bounding policy to it.

## What this ADR deliberately does NOT decide

The access and grant semantics for a subject, including what a channel grant means (ADR-0005, ADR-0012). The message model and how a sender tag renders to the agent as `[sender]: text` (ADR-0010). The outbound policy on the conduit (the budget-and-outbound-notifications ADR). Routing of an inbound to an agent (ADR-0003). The exact adapter registration API, the per-channel configuration and credential schema, and the registration and authentication procedure itself. The rest of the binding lifecycle beyond creation and operator revocation (rebinding and expiry). The group-conversation configuration (which subjects, and the scope semantics for a shared-reader message). Which platforms ship first (ADR-0009). Which node holds a channel's credentials (ADR-0013, ADR-0017). The storage, retention, inspection surface, and exact rate-limit or coalescing policy of the rejected-inbound and registration-attempt record (the audit role of ADR-0002).

## Consequences

A new channel is a new adapter to the contract plus a rebuild, with pao's core staying platform-neutral, the same shape ADR-0011 gave backends. Keying identity on (channel, per-sender id) rather than on the channel lets one bot serve several humans and one human span several platforms, which is the resolution ADR-0012 deferred, while the default-deny keeps an unbound sender both powerless and unable to probe past a uniform registration surface. Carrying a per-sender id even in a one-to-one makes a group conversation an extension by configuration rather than a separate code path, so the one-to-one and the many-human case share one mechanism, and a group that is not yet configured discloses to no one. Nothing a channel delivers is lost silently: an unknown or out-of-scope inbound leaves a full record to inspect.
